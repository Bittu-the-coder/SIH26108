## 1. The Problem — In Full Depth

### 1.1 What is actually going wrong

Every year, thousands of government departments, PSEs, and procurement agencies write technical specifications for tenders — for everything from steel pipes to hand sanitizers to CCTV cameras. Indian law and procurement policy require these specifications to reference the correct **Indian Standard(s) (IS)** published by the **Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS)**.

The catch: BIS has published **over 22,000 Indian Standards** across almost every product category, and:

- **Standards overlap in scope.** A single product (say, a steel almirah) can be touched by a material standard, a dimensional standard, a safety standard, and a testing-method standard simultaneously.
- **Standards get revised and superseded.** An IS number doesn't disappear when replaced — the old one is marked "superseded," but a procurement officer searching manually can easily cite the withdrawn version.
- **Standards cross-reference each other.** IS 796:2024, for example, explicitly supersedes IS 796:1966 and is itself referenced by at least four other standards (IS 11956, IS 9400, IS 9637). A specification that names only the "main" standard but omits its normative references is incomplete.
- **Amendments are issued separately from the base standard**, so "IS 2062" without checking for its latest amendment may already be technically wrong.
- **Some products fall under mandatory certification regimes** (BIS ISI mark, the Compulsory Registration Scheme/CRS for electronics, Hallmarking for gold jewellery, Quality Control Orders/QCOs for specific sectors) that must be cited *in addition to* the technical IS number — and procurement officers often don't know which regime applies to which product.

### 1.2 Why this happens (root cause)

It is **not a training problem** — it's a **search and synthesis problem**. BIS's own search tool ("Know Your Standards," at services.bis.gov.in) is a keyword/IS-number lookup: you must already know roughly what you're looking for. It does not understand a plain-English tender description like *"3-seater steel office chair with adjustable height for government office use"* and infer that this touches furniture dimension standards, powder-coating/finish standards, load-testing standards, and possibly BIS certification requirements. There is no semantic layer, no "also consider these five allied standards," and no automatic flag for "this standard was superseded in 2024."

### 1.3 Who is affected

| Stakeholder | Pain today |
| --- | --- |
| **Procurement officers** (primary user) — in ministries, PSEs, GeM sellers/buyers | Spend hours manually searching BIS's site, often non-experts in the specific engineering domain, under time pressure to publish a tender |
| **BIS itself** | Bears reputational and legal cost when disputes arise from ambiguous/incorrect tender specs |
| **Vendors/suppliers** | Face disqualification or disputes when tenders cite outdated or incomplete standards, or bid against ambiguous specs |
| **Auditors / CVC / courts** | Deal with procurement disputes and litigation traceable to specification errors |
| **The public** | Ultimately receives lower-quality goods/services when specs are wrong or incomplete |

### 1.4 Why it matters (impact of the problem, left unsolved)

- **Procurement disputes and litigation** — vendors challenge tenders citing incorrect/outdated standards, delaying public projects.
- **Quality degradation** — incomplete specs let substandard products pass, directly undermining BIS's consumer-protection mandate (this PS literally sits under the Department of Consumer Affairs).
- **Time and cost overruns** — every dispute or re-tender delays public spending (roads, hospitals, defence equipment, welfare-scheme goods).
- **Erosion of "Make in India" quality signalling** — inconsistent standard citation weakens the credibility of BIS certification as a quality mark, domestically and for export credibility.

## 2. Proposed Solution

### 2.1 One-line framing

A **Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) system** that takes a plain-language product description or tender document as input, retrieves the correct Indian Standards from a **verified, versioned knowledge base** (not the open internet), and returns a structured, cited recommendation — never a hallucinated one.

### 2.2 Why RAG, specifically, and not "just ask an LLM"

The PS's own core bottleneck (Step 7) is exactly this: **hallucination is unacceptable** when the output is a legal/procurement citation. An LLM asked "what IS standard applies to X" from parametric memory alone will confidently invent plausible-looking IS numbers. The only defensible architecture is one where:

1. The LLM **never generates an IS number from memory**.
2. Every recommended standard is **retrieved from a curated database** you built and can prove is sourced from BIS.
3. The LLM's only job is **semantic matching and explanation** — mapping "steel office chair" to the right retrieved candidates and explaining *why*, in plain language.

This "retrieval is ground truth, generation is explanation" separation is the single most important design decision in your PRD — jury members with any AI background will look for exactly this.

### 2.4 Scoped-down build plan for a solo/small team (realistic, not aspirational)

Given your strong ML/NLP background, here's what's actually buildable and demoable, versus what should stay as "future roadmap" in the PRD (jury respects an honest phased plan more than an overclaimed one):

**Phase 1 — Demo-able now (your actual submission scope):**

- A curated database of **500–2,000 IS standards** (not all 22,000) across 3–5 product categories relevant to common government procurement (e.g., furniture, textiles, electrical fittings, construction materials, safety equipment) — built from BIS's public metadata (titles, scope, classification group, supersession, cross-references) — see §4.
- Embedding-based semantic search (BGE-M3 or similar multilingual embedding model) over this metadata using pgvector or a lightweight vector store (Qdrant/Chroma).
- A cross-reference graph (even a simple adjacency table) capturing "supersedes" and "referred in" relationships scraped from the "Know Your Standards" detail pages.
- An LLM synthesis step that only summarizes/explains retrieved candidates — grounded prompting, with explicit instruction to say "no confident match found" rather than invent an IS number.
- Simple UI: text box in, structured recommendation card out, with a direct link to the BIS page for verification.
- Multilingual input handling via translation (e.g., IndicTrans2 or an LLM translation call) before retrieval.

**Phase 2 — Stated as roadmap, not built:**

- Full ingestion of all \~22,000 standards and amendments.
- Direct GeM portal integration (needs GeM's cooperation/API access — not public).
- OCR/parsing of uploaded tender PDFs at scale.
- Full CRS/QCO/Hallmarking rule engine per product category.
- Continuous sync when BIS publishes new standards/amendments.

Presenting it this way is itself a jury-scoring point: it directly answers **Step 6 (Jury Metrics)** on precision/recall and hallucination avoidance by showing you understand the tradeoffs, and **Step 5 (Constraints)** by showing you're not glossing over the "must not hallucinate" requirement.

## 4. Where to Find Real Data (verified sources)

This is the part most teams get wrong — they either scrape from unofficial/legally risky mirrors, or give up and use synthetic data. Use official sources; **cite them explicitly in your PRD**, since the jury will check.

### 4.1 Primary source: BIS "Know Your Standards" portal

**[https://www.services.bis.gov.in](https://www.services.bis.gov.in)** → "Know Your Standards" tab.

- Search by IS number, keyword, or ECO Mark.
- Each standard's detail page gives you (without needing to buy the full PDF): IS number, title, superseded/superseding standard, number of revisions/amendments, classification group/sub-group, and — critically — **the list of other Indian Standards that reference it** (the cross-reference graph you need for "allied standards").
- Note: standards published **after 1 Oct 2025** have moved to a *new* BIS portal — check the redirect link on the same page.
- Full-text PDFs of *indigenous* standards are downloadable **free after free registration** (see the official BIS "Steps to Download Indian Standards" guide) — but **only for personal/reference use**. Full-text reproduction/redistribution of IS documents is copyrighted by BIS; your dataset should be built from the **metadata** (titles, scope, classification, cross-references, supersession chains), not from redistributing full PDF text.

### 4.2 BIS "Schemes through Standards" mapping booklets

**[https://www.bis.gov.in](https://www.bis.gov.in)** → search "Mapping of Schemes/Missions of Government of India."

- BIS has already published \~29 ministry-wise PDF booklets mapping **3,200+ IS standards to 75 government schemes**, with mandatory (QCO-marked) standards highlighted.
- This is a goldmine for your **seed dataset**: it's already curated, ministry-tagged, and product-categorized — ideal for your Phase-1 scoped-down demo (§2.4). Start with 2–3 of these booklets (e.g., furniture/civil works, electrical, textiles) to build your first few hundred records.

### 4.3 Certification/compliance layer

- **Compulsory Registration Scheme (CRS)** — electronics/IT products, under MeitY + BIS: list of notified product categories is published on **bis.gov.in** under "Scheme-X Certification → Products under Compulsory Certification."
- **Hallmarking** — gold jewellery, under bis.gov.in → "Hallmarking" section (regulations, mandatory orders, list of hallmarking centres).
- **Quality Control Orders (QCOs)** — sector-specific mandatory-standard notifications, also listed per-standard on the "Know Your Standards" detail pages ("QCO File Details").
- Use these to build the "mandatory certification requirement" feature — a simple lookup table of {product category → certification scheme} is enough for a demo.

### 4.4 GeM (Government e-Marketplace) — for product-category vocabulary

**[https://gem.gov.in](https://gem.gov.in)** — no public open dataset/API for categories, but GeM publishes category counts and structure in its public presentations/reports (11,969 product categories as of Feb 2024). For your demo, you don't need live GeM integration — just use GeM's public category taxonomy (from their published reports) as a source of realistic *product description phrasing* to test your semantic matcher against.

### 4.5 What to avoid

Unofficial mirrors that host full IS standard text (e.g., archived/scraped copies) sit in a **contested legal grey zone** — BIS has historically objected to such redistribution. For a government hackathon submission, **do not build your pipeline around scraping these**; stick to BIS's own metadata and your own registered downloads for validation only.

## 5. Steps After You Have the Dataset

1. **Build the metadata schema first.** For each standard: `IS number, title, scope/classification group & sub-group, status (current/superseded/withdrawn), superseded-by, supersedes, cross-references (referred-in list), amendments, applicable certification scheme (if any), source URL`. This schema *is* your knowledge graph's node structure.
2. **Construct the cross-reference graph.** Even a simple `standard_A → normatively_references → standard_B` edge list (from the "referred in" data on each BIS detail page) is enough to power the "allied standards" feature — this doesn't need a graph database for a demo; a Postgres adjacency table is fine.
3. **Generate embeddings for semantic search.** Embed `title + scope + classification path` per standard using a multilingual model (BGE-M3 is a good open-weight choice for Hindi/English/regional-language parity). Store in pgvector or Qdrant.
4. **Build the hybrid retriever.** Combine dense (embedding) search with a keyword/BM25 pass over IS titles — pure semantic search alone tends to under-perform on exact product-name matches (e.g., "PVC pipes" should reliably surface IS 4985 even if the embedding similarity is middling).
5. **Add the grounding guardrail before the LLM step.** Never let the LLM see the whole standards database — only pass the top-K retrieved candidates into its context, and explicitly instruct it: *"Only reference standards provided in context. If none match with reasonable confidence, say no confident match was found."* This is your single biggest defence against hallucination and should be called out explicitly in your PRD.
6. **Design the output schema before the UI.** Recommend the IS number, its current status, allied/normative standards, and applicable certification — as structured JSON — before you touch frontend code. This makes the "Compliance Scorecard" and "Automated Citation Generator" killer features trivial to bolt on later, since they're just different renderings of the same structured output.
7. **Write 15–20 test queries by hand** spanning a few product categories, with the *correct* IS numbers you've manually verified from BIS. This becomes your evaluation set for precision/recall — exactly what Jury Metric Step 6 asks for — even if you never build the full system.
8. **For the PRD/pitch**, prepare one worked example end-to-end (e.g., "steel office chair" → matched IS + allied standards + certification flag) as your live or slide-based demo centerpiece.

## 6. Risks to Flag Honestly in Your PRD

- **Data completeness**: full-text IS documents are copyrighted; your system works on official metadata + registered-download validation, not open redistribution of standard text.
- **Legal liability**: an AI recommendation is *advisory*, not authoritative — final sign-off must remain with a human procurement officer. State this explicitly; juries in a government PS reward this kind of responsible-AI framing.
- **Coverage**: with \~22,000 total standards, a hackathon-scope system will only cover a curated subset — be upfront about this rather than implying full coverage.
